SIR Model of Infectious Diseases

Resource Library

What is the SIR Model?

The SIR model is one of the foundational frameworks of mathematical epidemiology. It divides a population into compartments that track how individuals move through different states of infection over time.

The Three Classic Compartments

  • S — Susceptible: Individuals who have never been infected and can contract the disease.
  • I — Infectious: Individuals who are currently infected and can transmit the pathogen to susceptible people.
  • R — Recovered: Individuals who have acquired immunity (through recovery or vaccination) and can no longer be infected or transmit.

Extension Used in This Portal

Ebola has a very high case-fatality rate, so this portal uses an extended SIRD structure with a fourth compartment:

  • D — Deceased: Individuals who died from the infection.

The Governing Equations

dS/dt = −β · S · I / N
dI/dt =  β · S · I / N − (γ + μ) · I
dR/dt =  γ · I
dD/dt =  μ · I

Where each symbol has a direct epidemiological meaning:

  • β (beta) — the transmission rate: how many effective contacts a single infectious individual makes per day, weighted by the probability of transmission per contact.
  • γ (gamma) — the recovery rate: the inverse of the mean infectious period. If the average person stays infectious for 10 days, γ = 1/10 = 0.1 per day.
  • μ (mu) — the mortality rate out of the infectious compartment.
  • N — total population size (S + I + R + D).

The Reproduction Number

The single most informative summary quantity is the basic reproduction number: 

R₀ = β / (γ + μ)

R₀ represents the expected number of secondary infections caused by one infectious individual in a fully susceptible population. If R₀ > 1, the outbreak grows; if R₀ < 1, it shrinks. Interventions aim to reduce the effective reproduction number Rₜ below 1.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Homogeneous mixing: all individuals contact each other with equal probability.
  • No latent period: newly infected individuals become infectious immediately.
  • Permanent immunity after recovery.
  • Closed population: no births, deaths from other causes, or migration.

These assumptions are approximations. In practice, models are calibrated against observed incidence and re-estimated as new data arrives — exactly what the Ebola Portal does daily.
